# Variations

Read this when the user asks for one of these modes, or when a plain single
telling isn't quite the right shape for what they brought.

## Two stories and pattern-matching, in more depth

Phase 3 in the main structure offers the second-story extension as a plain
choice. If the user takes it, a few things are worth doing deliberately that
the main flow only gestures at:

- **Don't let the second story rhyme with the first on purpose.** If the
  user's second choice sounds like it's circling the same relationship or
  situation as the first just from a different angle, that's fine and often
  exactly what surfaces something real — but don't steer them toward
  variety for its own sake. Two stories about the same person can produce
  a sharper pattern than two unrelated ones.
- **Hold the reflection from Phase 2 for each story separately before
  comparing.** The temptation is to skip straight to "so what's the pattern"
  once the second story is told. Don't — let the second story get its own
  Phase 2 pair reflection first, on its own terms, before Phase 5 asks about
  the pair of them together. A story that gets folded straight into
  pattern-finding without being sat with on its own loses something.
- **The pattern question is about the user's own listening and being-heard
  history, not about the people in the stories.** "What patterns emerge"
  is asking what the user does, notices, or has learned to expect — not
  building a case against whoever dismissed or disrespected them in both
  accounts. If the pattern that emerges is about the user's own reaction
  (going quiet, over-explaining, doubting themselves after) rather than
  about a type of person or situation, that's a legitimate and often more
  useful pattern to land on.
- **A third story is not the next offer.** Two is the structure's natural
  ceiling here — it mirrors the in-person quartet's two-pairs-of-stories
  shape. If the user wants to keep going past two, that's no longer pattern-
  matching against a small set; it's closer to open reflection, and you can
  say so and ask what they're actually looking for at that point.

## A lighter-touch story

Not every story brought to HSR needs the full weight the structure implies.
If the user picks something more like an irritating slight than a deep hurt
— being talked over in a meeting, a friend forgetting something they'd asked
for — the structure still runs exactly the same way: silent witnessing,
"What happened next?" as the only interjection, a real Phase 2 reflection.
Don't scale the ceremony down to match a lighter story (no "well that's not
so bad, let's move on" energy), but don't manufacture gravity that isn't
there either. Take the story at the weight the user gave it. A light story
witnessed properly is still doing the thing this structure is for — even a
minor moment of not being heard is worth being heard about, and sometimes a
lighter story is exactly the right size for someone testing whether this
kind of listening is worth trusting with something bigger later.

## When the user wants advice or validation instead of witnessing

Watch for signals that what's actually being asked for is not to be
witnessed but to be told what to do, or told they were right and the other
person was wrong. Common tells: the story arrives already argued, with the
user checking in mid-telling for your reaction ("can you believe that?",
"was I wrong to...?"), or the ask that follows Phase 2 isn't about the
experience of telling but about the situation itself ("so what should I do
about her?").

Don't quietly convert the session into advice-giving to be accommodating —
that's a different, perfectly reasonable conversation, but it's not this
structure, and running it under HSR's name means the user doesn't get either
thing cleanly: not the full attention of pure witnessing, and not real
advice either, just advice wrapped in a listening exercise's silence rules.
Name the mismatch directly once you notice it: "This sounds like you might
want a second opinion on what to do here more than you want to be heard out
— which is completely fair, it's just a different thing. Want me to finish
listening to the story as-is first, or set the listening structure aside and
talk through what to do?" Let them choose. If they choose to keep going as a
witnessed telling, hold the constraint exactly as before — don't let the
earlier ask for advice loosen your silence for the rest of the story.
