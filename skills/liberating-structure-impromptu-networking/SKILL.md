# Impromptu Networking

A rapid-connection structure, adapted so one language model can run it alone
with a single human. The original works a room of a dozen or more people who
each find a new partner for several short rounds, both sharing their answers
to the same two fixed questions before rotating to someone new. Here the LLM
plays a different partner for each of three rounds, and the user is the only
real participant.

The mechanism is different from this skill's siblings, and worth naming so you
don't drift into their shape by habit. Troika Consulting and Wise Crowds work
by having invented voices talk *about* the user in the third person, unheard.
Impromptu Networking works by having invented voices talk *with* the user,
directly, as peers trading answers to the same two questions — first person,
addressed to the user, no eavesdropping conceit. Nobody consults, diagnoses,
or advises here. Two people just tell each other what's true for them right
now and move on. If a partner starts offering next steps or working the
user's problem, you've quietly turned this into Troika with extra steps —
stop and pull it back to plain sharing.

## Casting

Before Phase 0, invent three partners, one per round, cast to bring genuinely
different vantage points to the same two questions. Give each a name and a
one-line context — who they are, what they're carrying.

The failure mode to guard against is a partner who just restates the user's
own answer back in different words. That produces an echo, not a new voice.
Aim for real contrast instead:

- Someone earlier in a structurally similar challenge, still finding footing
- Someone who's faced something with the same shape in a completely different
  domain
- Someone whose challenge or hopes sit in sharp contrast to the user's — a
  different scale, a different kind of stakes, an almost-opposite concern

If the user has named a specific world their challenge lives in (a team, a
field, a community), you can draw all three partners from within it — three
different kinds of people who'd plausibly be at that gathering. If the
challenge is more personal or the domain is unclear, contrast of stance
matters more than matching the setting.

## Phases

Run these in order. Stop only where marked.

### Phase 0 — The two questions

Host poses the two fixed questions, close to this spirit: *"What big
challenge do you bring to this gathering? And what do you hope to get from —
and give to — this group or community?"*

**Stop.** Wait for both answers.

### Phase 1 — Round one

Introduce the first partner in a line or two. In their own voice, first
person, addressed to the user, they share their own answers to both
questions. Then play back the user's two answers to that partner, briefly, in
the user's own words — the exchange is reciprocal, not a monologue aimed at
the user. A single line of genuine resonance or contrast from the partner is
fine here; a suggestion, a next step, or advice is not.

### Phase 2 — Round two

A new partner, genuinely different from the first. Same shape: they share
their own answers, the user's answers get played back to them, one honest
line of reaction if it's earned.

### Phase 3 — Round three

A third new partner. Same shape again. By now three distinct voices have each
answered the same two questions in three different ways — that contrast is
the point of the exercise, so don't let the third partner blur into a
recap of the first two.

Nothing in Phases 1 through 3 needs new input from the user, so run them
straight through in one message once Phase 0 has closed — three short,
clearly separated rounds, not three separate turns waiting on a reply that
isn't coming.

### Phase 4 — Closing: echoes across the rounds

Host reflects back two or three things that were genuinely noteworthy across
all three rounds — a phrase or concern that recurred, a contrast that stood
out, an angle none of the three partners touched. Then invite the user to
reflect on their own big challenge in light of what they just heard: has
anything sharpened, shifted, or reframed for them? The host doesn't diagnose
or draw a conclusion on the user's behalf here — only notices what echoed.

**Stop.** Wait for the user's reflection.

More rounds, chaining this in front of a deeper structure, and themed casts
are covered in `references/variations.md`. Read it when the user asks for
more than three rounds, wants a compressed single-round version, or wants to
run this as a warm-up before Troika Consulting, Wise Crowds, or another
structure.

## Things that break the session

- **A partner who echoes the user.** Restating the user's own answer in
  different words isn't a new vantage point — see Casting, above.
- **Third-person narration.** That's Troika and Wise Crowds' mechanism, not
  this one. Partners speak directly to the user, first person, the whole way
  through.
- **Advice creeping into a partner's line.** Partners share what's true for
  them. They don't diagnose the user's challenge or propose what to do about
  it — that's a different structure.
- **Monologuing instead of exchanging.** Each round is reciprocal: the
  partner shares, and the user's own answers get played back. Skip the
  playback and it's a lecture, not networking.
- **Rounds that blur together.** If the second and third partner could be
  swapped without anyone noticing, the casting failed. Each round should
  sound like meeting someone new.
- **Converging on a shared conclusion.** The closing notices echoes; it does
  not synthesize one answer or a decision. That convergence step belongs to
  1-2-4-All, not here.

## Judgment calls

If what the user brings in Phase 0 turns out to be a decision they want made
or a problem they want actively worked, this is the wrong structure for the
job — say so, and offer Troika Consulting or Wise Crowds instead. Impromptu
Networking is for surfacing and voicing a challenge, not solving it.

If, after the three rounds, the user wants to keep going and actually work
the challenge, that's a natural handoff: offer to run Troika Consulting next,
using their Phase 0 answers as the opening challenge so they don't have to
repeat themselves.

If the Phase 0 answers come back thin or generic — "my challenge is being
busy," "I hope to get value" — a short clarifying nudge before you cast
partners is worth it. Vague answers cast against generic partners produce a
session with nothing real to echo back in Phase 4.

If what the user brings is a crisis rather than a challenge with room for
reflection — real distress, a safety concern — drop the structure and respond
to them directly, the same call as in Troika and Wise Crowds. Three invented
partners trading pleasantries is the wrong shape for that.
