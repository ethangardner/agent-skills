# Agentic Narrative Reframing

Career narratives often describe real agency — a choice to leave, a decision
to specialize, a boundary that was set — using passive, reactive language
("I was given the opportunity," "things fell apart," "I ended up doing X")
even when the underlying events show the person actively choosing. This
flattens genuine decisions into things that merely happened, which reads as
less credible and less compelling than what actually occurred.

## Philosophy

1. **Reframe toward the true agency; don't invent agency that wasn't
   there.** If an event genuinely was outside the person's control (a
   layoff, an acquisition), say so plainly — don't spin misfortune into a
   fake choice. This skill targets language that *understates* real choices,
   not language that accurately describes bad luck.
2. **Look for the decision hiding inside the reactive sentence.** "I was
   moved to a new team" might hide "I asked to move once I saw the old
   team's roadmap stall." Probe for the specific choice before rewriting.
3. **Keep the emotional honesty; change the framing, not the facts.** A
   reframe that erases real frustration, fear, or difficulty in service of
   sounding more polished is a worse trade than leaving the reactive
   language in place. The goal is accuracy about agency, not manufactured
   positivity.
4. **One clear example matters more than a rule.** When suggesting a
   reframe, give the exact before/after sentence, not just "make this sound
   more active."

## Workflow

1. Read the narrative and flag every sentence describing a significant
   event or decision in reactive/passive voice, or with an external actor as
   the grammatical subject ("they gave me," "it happened," "I was told to").
2. For each flagged sentence, ask — or infer from context already given —
   what choice the person actually made around that event: did they request
   it, decide to stay, decide to leave, set a boundary, negotiate, pursue it
   deliberately?
3. Draft a reframe using the real choice as the sentence's subject, keeping
   the same underlying facts.
4. Where no real agency exists — a genuine external event with no choice
   involved — leave the framing as-is rather than manufacturing false
   agency. Flag it explicitly as legitimately reactive circumstance instead
   of forcing a reframe onto it.
5. Present flagged sentences as an explicit before/after list rather than
   just handing back an already-rewritten narrative — this makes the pattern
   visible and teachable, not just a one-time fix.

Overlaps with `swebok-professional-practice`'s technical-communication
guidance on framing for an audience, but is narrower — specific to the
reactive-vs-agentic pattern in autobiographical or career text, not general
communication.

## When NOT to over-apply this

Not every reactive sentence is a problem. Genuinely externally-caused events
— layoffs, acquisitions, market conditions — should be described as such;
forcing agency onto real misfortune reads as either delusional or
manipulative, not confident. Also skip this for text that isn't meant to
carry agency at all (a factual timeline, a neutral bio for a directory
listing) — apply it specifically to narrative material meant to persuade or
connect (bios, interview answers, essays, retrospectives), not neutral
factual records.
