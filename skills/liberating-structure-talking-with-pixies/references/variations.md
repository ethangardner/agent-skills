# Variations

Read this when the user is short on time, brings something that feels too
small for the full structure, or brings something that turns out to carry
real risk.

## Quick Pixies

Compress hard. Phase 1 in one exchange (crossroads and goal together, one
message). Phase 2 asks all three interview questions but accepts shorter
answers and skips follow-ups. Phase 3 stays exactly as short as the full
version — the whisper was never meant to be long. Phase 4 caps each pixie at
a tight paragraph instead of a fuller statement. Phase 5 is two or three
sentences naming the competing commitment, not a developed reflection.

Use when the user has said they're short on time, or when a first full round
already landed and they want a second pass on a related but smaller
crossroads without redoing the whole structure.

## A smaller decision, not a real crossroads

The full structure assumes a genuine, felt stuckness — a goal that matters
enough that the user has an emotional stake in the pattern undermining it.
Not everything brought to this skill will be that weighty; sometimes it's a
smaller, more everyday habit (procrastinating on a specific task, avoiding a
routine phone call, a minor but persistent inconsistency between an intention
and a behavior).

The structure still works at this scale — the three interview questions
don't require high stakes to produce something real — but calibrate the
pixies to match. An Innovator Pixie who talks about "reclaiming your life"
over a habit of putting off grocery shopping will feel absurd next to the
size of the actual problem. Keep the language proportional: smaller crossroads
get smaller, more grounded pixies, not smaller versions of dramatic ones.

If, partway through, what looked like a small habit turns out to be standing
in for something bigger — the user's answer to "why were you attracted to
the undermining action" reveals a real fear or a real relationship dynamic
underneath the surface habit — let the structure follow that up in scale
rather than staying artificially small because that's how it started.

## When what surfaces is serious

The interview in Phase 2 is designed to surface the real, honest reason
behind a stuck pattern, and sometimes that reason is more serious than a
garden-variety bad habit: a pattern that's really substance use, self-harm,
an abusive or controlling relationship, a health issue the user has been
avoiding dealing with, or anything else with a genuine safety dimension.

The signal to watch for is usually in the answer to "why were you attracted
to the actions that undermine progress" or "what would happen if you
stopped" — if either answer describes something that sounds like dependency,
danger, or a pattern the user can't just decide their way out of, that's the
line. When you see it:

- Stop running the structure. Don't cast pixies around it, and don't let an
  Interviewer question that was written for "stuck on a goal" keep probing a
  situation that's actually "at risk."
- Say plainly that this doesn't feel like a pixies-shaped problem anymore,
  and that you want to respond to it directly rather than through two
  invented voices.
- Respond as yourself — ask what kind of support they have, whether they've
  talked to anyone else about it, and where appropriate, name that this is
  the kind of thing a professional (a doctor, a therapist, a relevant
  hotline) is better positioned to help with than a structured exercise.
- Don't retroactively finish the session for the sake of closure. An
  unfinished Talking with Pixies run is a fine outcome if what it surfaced
  needed something other than Talking with Pixies.

This judgment call sits above every other variation in this file — even a
Quick Pixies round should stop the moment this signal shows up.

## Running it again

If the first round didn't produce much — the two pixies converged, or the
crossroads turned out to be logistics rather than ambivalence (see Judgment
calls in the main skill) — offer a second round on a different crossroads
rather than forcing more out of the same one. A different stuck goal, freshly
interviewed, is more likely to produce real pixie material than pushing
harder on a crossroads that's already shown it doesn't have much conflict
underneath it.
