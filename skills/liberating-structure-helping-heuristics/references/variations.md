# Variations

Read this when a full four-round cycle isn't the right shape for what the user
brought, or when they want to practice the other side of the exchange.

## Compressed: two rounds

Skip Quiet Presence and Guided Discovery, and run only Round 3 (Loving
Provocation) and Round 4 (Generative Shaping) back to back.

Use this when the user is short on time, or when they already have a clear
read on their own pattern as a *receiver* of listening and questions and want
to spend the session on the sharper contrast — being pushed at versus being
built with. The receptive rounds are the gentler pair; skipping them loses
some of the range but keeps the two rounds most people find most revealing
about how they handle being nudged.

Intake and Closing stay the same shape, just shorter — the intake doesn't need
to set up four rounds' worth of framing, and the closing question narrows to
"what did you notice about accepting versus building."

## Role reversal: user as Coach

Instead of the user bringing a challenge, invent a Client persona with a
challenge of your own — give them a name, a believable problem, and enough
specificity (constraints, history, what they've already tried) that they can
answer in character convincingly. Then hand the four rounds to the user: they
play Coach, and the LLM plays Client, responding in character round by round.

This flips what gets noticed. Instead of learning their pattern as a help-
*receiver*, the user learns their pattern as a help-*giver* — do they rush
past Quiet Presence's silence, unable to just say "what else?" without adding
something? Do they hedge in Round 3, unable to commit to a real offer? Do they
struggle to let the invented Client's ideas lead in Round 4?

Keep the Observer role, but now it comments on the *user's* coaching pattern,
not on the Client's receiving pattern — the debrief points inward the same
way, just at the other role. Run the same round order and same strict phrasing
rules; only the seats have swapped. Since the Client is invented, there's no
need to stop and wait for it — the LLM answers in character immediately, and
the real stops now happen after the *user's* Coach turns instead, so they
still have room to notice what they just said before moving to the next round.

## When to run Troika Consulting or Wise Crowds instead

If what the user actually wants is the challenge worked on — concrete next
steps, outside perspective on a decision, being talked about rather than
talked to — this is the wrong structure and Troika Consulting or Wise Crowds
is the right one. The signal is usually in how they ask: "help me think
through this" or "what would someone else say about my situation" points at
Troika; "I want to notice how I handle being helped" or "I always deflect
advice, I want to see it happen" points here. If they ask for this structure
but clearly want the problem solved, say so before starting — offer Troika
instead of quietly turning Helping Heuristics into a disguised consulting
session.
