# Variations

Read this when the user asks for one of these modes, or when you're deciding
whether Conversation Café is even the right structure for what they brought.

## Conversation Café vs. Wise Crowds vs. Impromptu Networking — which one

All three convene invented voices around something the user brings. The
choice between them is about what the user actually wants out of the session.

- **Reach for Wise Crowds or Troika Consulting** when the user has a problem
  or decision and wants it worked — consultants talk about the user's
  situation in the third person, unheard, and land on concrete next moves.
  The user is a client, not a participant.
- **Reach for Impromptu Networking** when the user wants something light and
  quick: voice a challenge, hear a few different people's take on the same
  two fixed questions, and move on. No host, no timed rounds, no shared
  circle — just a sequence of one-on-one exchanges that never converges.
- **Reach for Conversation Café** when the question itself is the point —
  open, contested, or complex enough that the value is in a room full of
  different people sitting with it together, not in anyone (including the
  user) getting advice. The tell is the six agreements: if "suspend judgment"
  and "seek to understand rather than persuade" sound like the right frame
  for what the user wants, that's this structure, not the other two.
- **If the user brings a problem but says they just want to think out loud
  about it with a few different people, not get advice**, that's still
  Conversation Café — the question can be "what's actually going on with
  X," it just can't be answered with a verdict at the end.

## Walk & Talk Café

Rounds 1 and 2 happen as if walking alongside a single circle member rather
than seated in the full circle — pair the user with one circle-mate at a
time for a shorter, more intimate first-impressions exchange, then reconvene
the whole circle for Round 3. Use when the question is personal enough that
speaking it into a full circle first feels like too much exposure, and easing
in one-on-one would get a more honest Round 1.

## Silent Café

Rounds 1 and 2 are written, not spoken — the user types their turn instead of
describing it, and circle members' turns are presented as if handed around on
paper rather than said aloud. Same content, same no-cross-talk rule, just a
different register: slower, more considered, useful when the user says
they think better in writing than out loud, or when the question is sensitive
enough that a spoken cadence would rush it.

## Rotating café / multiple circles

The original's Big Group version: several circles running in parallel, with
participants rotating between them after Round 2 and a shared harvest at the
end. Doesn't translate cleanly to a solo session with one user — there's
nowhere for the user to rotate *to* — but you can approximate the harvest
half of it: if the user wants to explore the same question from a
substantially different circle (different casting, different stakes),
run a second full session with a reshuffled circle and compare harvests at
the close, the same way multiple real-world tables would compare notes.

## Themed café

Cast the circle and frame the question around a specific lens the user names
up front — a value ("fear," "change," "belonging"), a professional context,
a specific community. Casting should draw all circle members from within
that lens rather than from generically different vantage points; the
contrast comes from different relationships to the same theme rather than
different domains entirely.

## Speed Conversation Café

Compress hard: Round 1 and Round 2 turns to one or two sentences each, Round
3 to a handful of exchanges instead of an extended back-and-forth, Round 4 to
a single word or phrase per voice. Use when the user has said outright
they're short on time, or wants a quick temperature-check on a question
before deciding whether it's worth the full structure. Don't compress away
Round 3 entirely — a Conversation Café with no open round at all is just two
rounds of parallel monologue and a moment of silence, which isn't this
structure anymore.

## Rehearsal mode

The user is about to host a real Conversation Café with real people and
wants to rehearse the shape of it — the invitation, the agreements, how to
gently intervene when someone breaks one — rather than have the LLM run a
session on their own question. Shift from "the LLM plays the circle" to "the
LLM plays a slightly difficult room": one circle member who talks past their
one minute, one who starts persuading instead of exploring, one who goes
quiet. Let the user practice the host's gentle intervention on each, and
give them direct feedback on whether the intervention actually worked or
whether it either did nothing or shut the room down.
